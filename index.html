Core Mathematical Framework & Astrodynamic Optics

Lunar polar heliostats deployed on crater rims (e.g., Shackleton, Shoemaker) operate under grazing solar illumination ($0.5^\circ \le \beta \le 1.8^\circ$). Unilateral solar radiation heats one face of the 20-meter composite mast to $+120^\circ\text{C}$ while the shadowed back chills to $-130^\circ\text{C}$, producing structural thermal camber. SolarReflect establishes a real-time closed-loop state inversion between crater thermal power density ($E$) and drive gimbal phase ($\theta$).

1. Mast Thermal Camber & Angular Tilt

Differential solar flux absorption across the tubular mast cross-section induces a temperature gradient $\Delta T(z)$. Euler-Bernoulli beam theory with thermal strains determines mirror gimbal angular misalignment $\theta_{\text{warp}}$:

$$\kappa(z) = \frac{\alpha_{\text{CTE}} \cdot \Delta T(z)}{D}, \quad \theta_{\text{warp}} = \int_0^L \kappa(z)\,dz = \frac{\alpha_{\text{CTE}} \cdot \overline{\Delta T} \cdot L}{D}$$
$\alpha_{\text{CTE}} = 1.2 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ (CFRP Ultra-Low Expansion Mast)
$L = 20.0\ \text{m}$ (Mast Height) | $D = 0.35\ \text{m}$ (Mast Outer Diameter)
$\overline{\Delta T} \approx 180\ \text{K}$ (Sunlit/Shadow Differential across wall)
Resulting Uncorrected Gimbal Tilt: $\theta_{\text{warp}} \approx 1.23\ \text{mrad}$ ($254\ \text{arcsec}$)

2. Crater Target Flux Convolution ($E$)

The heliostat projects a Gaussian-convolved solar disc image into the Permanently Shadowed Region (PSR). Crater surface irradiance $E(\theta)$ is governed by gimbal mechanical phase $\theta$ and optical throw distance $R$:

$$E(\theta) = E_0 \cdot \eta_{\text{refl}} \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{4\ln(2) \cdot [2R(\theta - \theta_0 - \theta_{\text{warp}})]^2}{w_{\text{spot}}^2} \right)$$
$E_0 = 1361\ \text{W/m}^2$ (Solar Constant at 1 AU)
$\eta_{\text{refl}} = 0.94$ (Silver-dielectric protected multilayer mirror)
$R = 2400\ \text{m}$ (Heliostat to Crater Floor Target Range)
At $1.2\ \text{mrad}$ error: Beam center drifts by $2.88\ \text{m}$, shedding 88% flux

3. Live Correlation Observer & Inversion

Rather than relying on open-loop encoders prone to mast bending and backlash, the sentry computer continuously correlates crater receiver telemetry $(E)$ with commanded mechanical phase $(\theta)$:

$$\Delta \hat{\theta}_{\text{warp}} = \arg\max_{\delta} \int_{t-\tau}^t E(t') \cdot \mathcal{S}\left(\theta(t') - \delta\right) dt'$$
$\mathcal{S}(\theta)$: Nominal symmetric synthetic spread function template
$\tau = 4.2\ \text{s}$: Sliding estimation window with recursive gradient ascent
Backlash isolation: Directional sign hysteresis gate $\operatorname{sgn}(\dot{\theta})$
Closed-loop pointing stability: $\le 12\ \mu\text{rad}$ ($2.47\ \text{arcsec}$)
